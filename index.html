I work at the meeting point of ultrafast optics and quantum many-body theory — using femtosecond light to drive quantum materials out of equilibrium, and building the theory needed to read what comes back. I recently completed the Erasmus Mundus QuanTEEM master's, which took me through Aarhus University, RPTU Kaiserslautern and Université Bourgogne Europe.

My route here was unusual. I started in high-energy theory in Bogotá, working on axion dark matter and thermal leptogenesis, before moving into condensed matter and quantum technologies. That background still shapes how I approach problems: I am comfortable moving between an optical table and an analytic calculation.

I am currently looking for a PhD position in strongly correlated and quantum many-body systems, hybrid quantum systems, and quantum technologies.

Jan – Jun 2026

Ultrafast dynamics and light-induced phase transitions in tellurium

Associate Research Student · Ultrafast Dynamics of Quantum Materials Group, Aarhus University — Supervisor: Prof. Simon Wall

  • Ran table-top pump–probe and rotational-anisotropy spectroscopy in the near-infrared, under ultra-high vacuum and cryogenic conditions.
  • Extracted coherent phonon frequencies, amplitudes and damping rates from linear and non-linear reflectance data by fitting time-domain signals to Raman- and IR-active phonons in a tensor model.
  • Worked with second-harmonic generation and sum- and difference-frequency generation in crystals on free-space optical setups.
  • Applied group-symmetry analysis to the dielectric function and the non-linear susceptibility tensor for arbitrarily oriented crystals, characterising phonon spectra and optical anisotropy.
  • Pump–probe spectroscopy
  • Coherent phonons
  • Second-harmonic generation
  • Rotational anisotropy
  • Cryogenics and UHV
  • Tellurium

Research Intern · Center for Complex Quantum Systems, Aarhus University — Supervisor: Prof. Georg Bruun
